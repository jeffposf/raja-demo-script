RAJA soutenance — Demo script & talk track (13/10/2026)

Duration: 1h30 demo, 4 acts after the intro, all 5 RAJA use cases covered (no imposed order). The wrap-up is carried by the sales team.

Act Time Use cases
0. Intro 5 min —
1. Jean-Christophe's journey: website, email, back to the website 25 min UC3, UC2, UC4 (partial)
2. Behind the scenes: how a business user builds it 30 min UC1, UC2, UC3
3. The same customer in the CRM 5 min UC2
4. Testing and measuring 15 min UC5
Buffer 5 min

RAJA use cases (Vendor Demonstration Guide): UC1 How to set up · UC2 Integrations and cross-channel usage · UC3 Personalization possibilities · UC4 Product hierarchy, variants and recommendation deduplication · UC5 Recommendation testing, measurement and incrementality.

How to read each screen: Show = what you click · Say = talk track · UC = use case covered.

Act 0 — Intro (5 min)

Screen 0.1 — Architecture slide

Show: the HLD slide.

Say: "Today I'm going to show you one customer, Jean-Christophe, a B2B buyer at Nova Logistique, and follow him across three visits to a RAJA website, an email, and your CRM. Everything you'll see runs live on Salesforce Personalization, on top of Data 360, the customer data platform. The website is a demo copy of raja.fr, with a catalog of 283 products in 50 families. I'll show you the journey first, as the customer sees it. Then I'll open the hood and show you how a business user builds it, without code. Then email and CRM. Then testing. I'll be clear about what is native and what is a workaround: you'll hear me say it each time."

Act 2 — Behind the scenes: how a business user builds it (30 min)

Switch to Salesforce. Never click Save in this act: every build is shown, not saved.

Screen 2.0 — One platform

Show: App Launcher → type "Sales" (don't open it), then "Personalization" → open the Personalization app.

Say: "Before I show you how Jean-Christophe's experience was built, let me briefly present the platform. Salesforce Personalization is built on the same technology as the core product, the CRM you're using, and it uses Data 360 as its data layer. It's one platform. Sales, Personalization: the foundation is the same. The apps are built for different business users, but the data is the same and flows into every app. So let me introduce you to Salesforce Personalization, the personalization engine of Salesforce."

UC: UC1, UC2.

Screen 2.1 — The three building blocks (slide or whiteboard)

Show: nothing on screen yet, or a simple drawing: a salesperson, a shelf, an instruction card.

Say: "So how does this personalization engine work? With three blocks: the recommender, the personalization point and the personalization decision. Think of a shop. The recommender is the salesperson: he picks the products. What people buy together, similar items, or it's an AI that does it. It answers the question: which products? Then the personalization point: it's where I want to display the products. On the home page, the cart page, the product page. It answers the question: where? Finally, the personalization decision answers the question: to whom? Everyone? Frequent buyers? Now let's answer each of these questions. First, which products: the recommender."

UC: UC1.

Screen 2.2 — The AI recommender

Show: Recommenders tab → "RAJA ML add to cart V2" → Edit. 1. Recommender properties: profile data graph "RAJA Profile RT", item data graph, fallback → Next. 2. Objective-based vs rule-based; objective = the custom add-to-cart objective → Next. 3. Filters, Include tab: add a condition to show it, Direct Attributes → Is Sellable → Static → Is Equal To → Yes. Delete the condition right after. Exclude tab: the existing filter Product Id / Decision Context / Match Any / Product Id. 4. Simulation: Sample Recipient = Jean-Christophe's Unified Individual ID, no Anchor Item ID → Simulate. Then Anchor Item ID = PR-001-01 → Simulate: PR-001-01 is no longer in the results. 5. Leave without saving. This is the live recommender: never Save & Exit.

Say: "Recommender. First question: which products? Here I select the customer data the engine learns from, the real-time profile, and my catalogue. I can also select a fallback: if for a given person the engine only finds two products, the fallback completes the rest of the zone. Now it gets interesting: you have objective-based, machine learning, and rule-based. Let's focus on objective-based: it uses machine learning to analyse behaviour and show an individualised recommendation. I give the machine learning a goal, here maximise add to cart. You can maximise revenue, clicks, or a goal you define from your own signals, like add to cart here. You decide what's best for your marketing strategy. On the filter screen, I can include: for instance, only recommend products that are sellable. And I can exclude: here I'm excluding the product I'm looking at right now. There's no sense in recommending something I'm already seeing. And finally, the simulation. I pick Jean-Christophe: here are his products. Now I add the product he's looking at, PR-001-01: it's gone from the list, the exclusion works. What matters is that I simulate the recommendation before it goes live on the website. If it's all good, I save. If not, I go back and change the settings."

UC: UC1, UC3.

Screen 2.3 — Personalization point

Show: Personalization Points tab → New: profile data graph, name, personalization type, content schema. Don't save. Then open "Home Recommended For You V4".

UC: UC1.

Screen 2.4 — Personalization decision

Show: "Home Recommended For You V4" → decision "ML Frequent Buyer V4" (priority 1) → Edit → decision config, then targeting rule (RAJA Avg Basket > order_count, Is Greater Than Or Equal To 10). The second decision is "For You All Visitor V4" (priority 2, "RAJA For You V2").

Say: "We saw which products and where to display them. Now let's see to whom, with the personalization decision. Decision config: here is the title of the zone you saw on the home page, and here I plug in the recommender, the machine learning one we just saw. Then the targeting rule: here I show this zone only to visitors with 10 orders or more, a calculated insight in Data 360. I can add more conditions to the rule: a segment such as top spenders, or any data available. And everyone else? They fall to the second decision, priority 2, with the rule-based best sellers we saw. One decision per visitor: the first one that matches wins. That's how real-time personalized content is configured on the web."

UC: UC1, UC3.

Screen 2.5 — Experience templates (only if asked)

Show: Experience Templates tab → "Product Recommendations Template" → Edit. Don't save.

Say: "Great question: how do we define the look and feel, and how is the content zone actually rendered? Through experience templates. I give it a name, then I select the website it's built for. I can start from a Salesforce global template or from scratch. Then I select the content schema, 'Recommendations - Product'. Remember, we saw it in the personalization point, and below are the fields the website gets back. Each field becomes a variable, and I insert the ones I want: the price or not, for instance. Then in the HTML I set the look and feel: font, colour, size. The product image comes from the variable, so it follows the recommendation. Once activated, this is the template I pick in a campaign or in the Web Personalization Manager, as you'll see."

If a technical profile asks: views and clicks are tracked by default on the template (option "Turn off auto-engagement tracking"), and that's what feeds the test results in Act 4.

Act 4 — Testing and measuring (15 min)

Screen 4.1 — The experiment, then product management

Show: Experiments tab → "PDP Also Bought Co-Buy vs ML" (on personalization point "PDP Customers Also Bought"): "Business as Usual" (control) and "ML add to cart", 50/50; primary metric RAJA Add To Cart, secondary Purchase and Product View. Then the two example dashboard captures (ABNCommerceRecsSplit). Then hand over to product management.

Say: "How do you know the AI does better? You test it, directly on the zone. Business as usual is our control group, versus the ML recommender. The primary metric, the one that decides the winner, is add to cart. Product views and purchases are secondary metrics: we follow them, but they don't decide. These dashboards are examples from another environment: our test started on 6 October and needs at least 14 days. Lift is the gain versus control. Chance to beat control is how sure we are, and we set the bar at 95% before we call a winner. Now our product management team will take over and share their screen to show you dashboards on A/B/n tests."
