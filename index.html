Act 0 — Intro (5 min)

Screen 0.1 — Architecture slide

Show: the HLD slide.

Say: "Today I'm going to show you one customer, Jean-Christophe, a B2B buyer at Nova Logistique, and follow him across three visits to a RAJA website, an email, and your CRM. Everything you'll see runs live on Salesforce Personalization, on top of Data 360, the customer data platform. The website is a demo copy of raja.fr, with a catalog of 283 products in 50 families. I'll show you the journey first, as the customer sees it. Then I'll open the hood and show you how a business user builds it, without code. Then email and CRM. Then testing. Screen 1.5 — Variant table (UC4, honest moment)

Show: scroll back up to "Tableau des références", select reference CAS01A.

Say: "This is RAJA's reality: one product, many references. Use case 4 asks how the platform handles that. Here is the honest answer. Salesforce Personalization has no built-in parent/variant hierarchy today. We worked around it with data: every product carries its family, and the 'similar products' recommender is told to keep only one product per family. That's configuration, no code. The 'no duplicates between the two zones' part is different: it's done on the website, in a few lines of JavaScript, because the platform removes duplicates inside one zone but not across two zones. We flagged both points in our answer, and they're on our list for the product team."

(If the demo device gets the "ML add to cart" variation and it lists several sizes of one box:) "You can see the limit here: this zone is currently in an A/B test, and the AI version doesn't know about families, so it shows several sizes of the same box. Act 2 — Behind the scenes: how a business user builds it (25 min)

Switch to Salesforce, Personalization app.

Screen 2.1 — The three building blocks (slide or whiteboard)

Show: nothing on screen yet, or a simple drawing: a salesperson, a shelf, an instruction card.

Say: "Everything you saw is built with three building blocks. If you remember three words from today, it's these. Think of a shop. One: the recommender. That's the salesperson who picks products. Each salesperson has a method: one picks 'what people buy together', another picks 'similar products', another one is an AI that learns from each customer. A recommender answers one question: which products? Two: the personalization point. That's a spot in the shop window: the hero banner, the 'customers also bought' zone, the cart zone. A personalization point answers: where? Three: the personalization decision. That's the instruction card stuck on the shelf: 'for frequent buyers, use the AI salesperson; for everyone else, use the interests salesperson'. A decision answers: for whom, and with what? Recommender, point, decision. Which products, where, for whom."

UC: UC1.

Screen 2.2 — Recommenders

Show: Recommenders list (8 recommenders). Open RAJA Co-Buy, then RAJA ML add to cart V2.

Say: "Here are our salespeople. Two kinds. Rule-based ones, like Co-Buy: we tell it 'rank products by how often they're bought together with the product on screen'. It's transparent: you can explain every result. And AI ones, like 'ML add to cart': we give it a goal, here 'maximise add-to-cart', and a deep-learning model finds the products most likely to reach that goal for this person. It can also recommend new products from their description, before anyone has bought them. On top of the AI, the marketer keeps control with filters. An inclusion filter says 'only recommend products that…', an exclusion filter says 'never recommend products that…'. Here we have one exclusion: never recommend the product the customer is looking at right now. Without it, the AI could suggest the very box he already has on screen. Each recommender can have a backup: if it can't find enough products, it falls back to top sellers. The zone is never empty."

UC: UC1, UC3.

Screen 2.4 — Personalization point and decisions

Only one per zone, never a mix. The rules can use anything in the customer profile: number of orders, segments, interests, the page, dates."

Act 4 — Testing and measuring (10 min)

Screen 4.1 — The experiment

Show: Experiments tab → "PDP Also Bought Co-Buy vs ML" (on personalization point "PDP Customers Also Bought"): two variations 50/50, "Business as Usual" (control, "Defer response to the Personalization Decisions") and "ML add to cart"; primary metric RAJA Add To Cart, secondary Purchase and Product View.

Say: "How do you know the AI does better than a simple rule? You test it. On the product page zone 'customers also bought', half the visitors get the current logic, Co-Buy. The other half get the AI recommender. We measure add-to-cart first, purchases and product views second. The current logic is called 'Business as Usual'. Technically, that group 'defers': it gets no test recommender and simply sees what the zone shows anyway. Results are read the Bayesian way: Salesforce tells you the probability that the AI version beats the current one. You need at least 1,000 people per group before it declares a winner, so on our demo site there's no result yet."

UC: UC5.

Screen 4.2 — What's not there yet (honest moment)

Show: stay on the experiment.

Say: "Use case 5 asks for incrementality: what do recommendations add compared to no recommendations at all, in euros. Two honest limits today. One: every test group must show something. You can compare 'new logic versus current logic', but not 'recommendations versus nothing'. Two: results are a percentage lift on counts, add-to-cart, purchases, views. Not extra revenue in euros. For that you'd export the data to your BI. Both are on our list for the product team, and our product management team is here if you want to go deeper."

UC: UC5.
